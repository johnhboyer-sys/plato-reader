Who speaks: test run, Republic I–II and Phaedo

Republic Books I–II and the whole Phaedo, labelled with the prompt as fixed after the pilot audit, by Claude Opus 5.5 through the Message Batches API: two independent passes over the Greek, code checks, then a third look at every row where the passes disagreed or a check fired. Each chip marks where a voice begins in Burnet's Greek. Grey italic is the narrator telling the story; a dotted underline is a voice quoted inside someone else's speech.

Each card shows the row under review in full, with the row before and after it faded, for context. The Greek is on the left and Lamb's or Shorey's English on the right. The chips in the English mark where each Greek voice change falls in the translation; a separate, quick model pass placed them, so if an English chip looks wrong, judge by the Greek.

What the test found

  • Every error from the pilot audit is fixed in the new Book I labels: the bare lead-ins at 328a1 and 328b2 now fold the same way, Polemarchus keeps 331d8 in both passes, Οὐκ ἔφη (346c1) is narration in both, and the Sophocles exchange (329a) splits cleanly.
  • The two passes disagreed on 3 Republic rows and 2 Phaedo rows, all over where a lead-in ends (Ὁ οὖν Κέβης ὑπολαβών, / Καὶ ὁ Ἀδείμαντος ἔφη·) or where Pindar's words start; none over who speaks.
  • Hackett's running heads (never shown to the model) agree with the labels on 24 of 26 spreads. The two others are expected: the opening spread's head leaves out Polemarchus, Adeimantus, Glaucon, and the boy, and Glaucon's long speech (358e–361b) has no words from Socrates.
  • Where Socrates addresses someone by name, the next reply came from that person in 68 of 76 turns; the other 8 are correct readings (Glaucon breaking in at 347a, Cebes answering a remark to Simmias, Crito nodding instead of replying).
  • Three Phaedo speeches, Socrates' last words among them, first failed to anchor because Burnet sets a dash with no space before them (τρίχας—Αὔριον). The extraction now splits the dash off, and all three resolve.
  • Flags left: Glaucon's and Adeimantus' quotations of poets adapted into their own syntax, and Adeimantus voicing the calculating young man (366b), which is labelled as imagined, as ruled.
